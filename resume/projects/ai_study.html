Independent Study — Development of AI Agents

A semester-long independent study spent testing what modern AI tools can actually do, not just what they claim to do — from building experimental agents to presenting a workshop on the results.

Overview

For my independent study, I set out to research how modern AI agent frameworks actually work and where their real limits are, rather than take the marketing claims at face value. That meant reading through current frameworks, synthesizing what I found into a structured technical report, and then testing a lot of it hands-on.

Testing AI's Real Capabilities

Rather than just reading about what these tools could do, I gave AI a series of genuinely hard problems and watched how far it could actually get. That included having it help build a custom virtual machine and compiler, put together a constrained instruction computation system, and stand up an experimental database server written in Fortran — each one chosen because it was well outside the kind of boilerplate code AI tends to handle easily.

I also explored the more practical, everyday side of AI tooling: automating workflows with Power Automate, and trying out platforms like Replit and Synthesia to see how well they held up on real tasks versus demos.

Building With Agents

Alongside the research, I built experimental AI agents in Python using the Gemini API, handling the data collection and preprocessing that went into them and tracking the work through GitHub version control.

Sharing what I learned: I presented a two-session workshop to classmates on building AI agents using both no-code and code-based approaches, which was well received and pushed me to explain technical ideas clearly to a non-specialist audience.

What I Learned

This study changed how I think about AI as a tool — it's genuinely capable in some areas and surprisingly limited in others, and the only way to know which is which is to actually test it. It also gave me real practice turning technical research into something I could explain clearly, both in writing and in front of a room.
